The gap

The hard part starts after detection.

SIEM, XDR, and AI SOC tools can confirm something real happened. Then the answer scatters across identity, cloud, SaaS, data, Slack, spreadsheets, and memory. InaiSec gives the response team a place to collect the facts, name the gaps, and make the next decision deliberately.

How it works

Bring one incident. Pressure-test the response path.

The review is structured enough to be useful, but plain enough that you do not need a prep packet. Bring the messy version, sanitized if needed.

  1. 1

    Bring the incident

    Start with the incident narrative: the alert, the identity or system involved, the data or customer concern, and the decision that felt harder than it should have.

  2. 2

    Map the evidence path

    We identify where the answer should have come from: identity, SaaS, source control, cloud, data stores, ticketing, chat, counsel notes, and human memory.

  3. 3

    Separate fact from judgment

    We mark what is known, what is missing, what is merely possible, and where a person needs to make a call rather than let a tool imply one.

  4. 4

    Decide whether to go deeper

    If there is a fit, we define what a design-partner working session should prove, what data can stay sanitized, and what readiness gates must exist before real access.

FAQ

Short answers.

Prefer email? hello@inaisec.ai

Who is a good design partner?

Security and IR leaders at customer-data companies who have handled a credential, SaaS, cloud, or data incident where impact was hard to prove.

Do we need production credentials?

No. The first review can use a tabletop or sanitized incident. Real access is only considered after readiness gates are reviewed together.

How is this different from AI SOC analysts?

AI SOC tools help triage alerts. InaiSec focuses on the room after an incident is confirmed: evidence, impact, accountable decisions, and the record.

What should we bring?

A recent, realistic, or sanitized incident: what triggered it, which identities or systems were involved, what was hard to prove, and who needed to decide.

What do we leave with?

A clearer evidence map, the open impact questions, named decision owners, and a practical next-step plan if deeper design-partner work makes sense.

Is this legal advice?

No. InaiSec is decision support for incident teams and counsel. Your legal team owns legal interpretation, approval, and notification decisions.

What does InaiSec not do?

It does not replace detection, containment, counsel, or human approval. The first review is about evidence, impact, and decision clarity after an incident is already real.
