The gap after detection

Everything after the alert is still spreadsheets, Slack, and memory.

Detection has never been better. SIEM, XDR, and AI SOC tools will tell you — with growing confidence — that something real happened. Then the actual work starts: which systems did the credential touch, what data was reachable, which customers are affected, which laws apply, and who signs their name to each of those answers.

In most teams that work still means pulling logs by hand from each console, a timeline built in a spreadsheet, and counsel joining a bridge call before the blast radius is clear. The gaps are the same four every time.

Evidence
What do we actually have — and what is missing, stale, or disputed?
Impact
Which systems, which data, which customers — confirmed, or merely possible?
Decision
Who owns the next call, and on what evidence basis?
Record
Can the story be reconstructed for leadership, customers, counsel, and auditors?

Meanwhile the clocks became real. GDPR gives 72 hours to notify the supervisory authority. The SEC gives a public company four business days from its materiality determination. Neither waits for the spreadsheet.

One incident, start to finish

How an investigation runs.

This is the tabletop scenario design partners run first: a GitHub personal access token leaked through CI/CD. Five steps — and the ones that matter stop and ask a person.

  1. 1

    Open the room

    A confirmed incident becomes an Incident Room: one surface for evidence status, timeline, blast radius, obligations, and decisions. Triage stays in your detection stack — incidents arrive here confirmed.

  2. 2

    Approve the evidence plan human checkpoint

    The Smart Connector proposes what to retrieve from each system the credential touched: Okta sessions, GitHub audit events, Snowflake query history. An analyst reviews the plan and approves it before anything runs. Retrieval is read-only, and every call to your systems is logged.

  3. 3

    Watch the blast radius form

    Evidence lands and the timeline threads itself — sessions correlated, anomalies flagged, gaps marked as gaps. Blast radius maps credential to systems to data to customers, each entry labeled confirmed or potential. Unknown counts show as pending — never filled in as zero.

  4. 4

    Read the obligations

    Blast-radius facts run through a deterministic rules engine — not a model. Four legally-reviewed regulations ship today: California §1798.82, New York SHIELD §899-aa, GDPR Article 33, and SEC Item 1.05. Clocks run where a statute sets one; where it does not, no countdown is invented. Jurisdictions outside coverage are flagged as explicit gaps, never silently dropped.

  5. 5

    Decide, sign, close human checkpoint

    Materiality is decided by a person, under their authenticated name, with a written rationale — that decision is what starts the SEC clock. Legal signs the notification packet before anything leaves the building; your team sends it, InaiSec records it. Closing the case purges the collected evidence and keeps the tamper-evident record.

Working software

What runs today. What does not yet.

InaiSec is pre-launch. The Phase 1 platform is built and runs, start to finish, in demo and tabletop form — and this page names what runs and what does not.

  • Evidence on demand

    Read-only retrieval from Okta, GitHub, Microsoft Entra ID, Google Workspace, AWS CloudTrail, and Snowflake, plus a configurable connector for custom APIs.

  • Blast radius and timeline

    Identity resolution across systems, session threading, anomaly and gap markers, credential-to-impact mapping with confidence levels.

  • Deterministic notification engine

    Four legally-reviewed regulations with obligation timers, statutory content elements, and draft packets. Coverage gaps are flagged, never guessed.

  • Human decision checkpoints

    Six first-class gates — scope, retrieval, impact, root cause, disclosure posture, communications — each with an owner and an append-only approval history.

  • Tamper-evident record

    Hash-chained activity log; every determination recorded under an authenticated principal.

  • Close with purge

    Evidence deleted on close with a verifiable purge manifest. The case record and audit trail stay.

Not built yet — and we say so

  • Containment

    Response actions stay in your existing tooling until the investigation layer earns trust. Phase 2 — and when it comes, it will not be LLM-generated.

  • DORA, NIS2, HIPAA, PCI, other US states

    In the schema, not yet legally reviewed. Until then they surface as explicit coverage gaps with “obtain local counsel” guidance.

  • Real-credential pilots

    Gated behind security readiness — tenant isolation, revocation, purge, audit — reviewed with each partner. Tabletop and sanitized pilots come first.

The rules we don't break

Five commitments, written into the architecture.

  • No data lake.

    Evidence is collected when an investigation needs it and purged when the case closes. Your logs are never pre-ingested, indexed, or retained just in case.

  • The LLM never sees credentials.

    Connections live in a scoped, encrypted vault. Models plan retrievals; deterministic code executes them. Revoking a credential halts in-flight retrievals.

  • Notification math is not AI.

    The engine that reads statutes is a versioned, legally-reviewed rules engine. Same facts in, same obligations out — every time, with citations.

  • Unknown is never zero.

    Missing evidence renders as missing. Counts that are not known read “pending”. A clean outcome reads “no evidence of data compromise” — which is not the same claim as “nothing happened”.

  • People own the decisions.

    Agents assemble and cite evidence. An accountable person approves scope, impact, materiality, and every packet — under their own name, with the rationale on record.

FAQ

Straight answers for early conversations.

Prefer email? hello@inaisec.ai

What is identity-first incident response?

Incident response that starts from the credential — the account, role, and access path — rather than from an endpoint alert. It answers what the identity touched, what data was reachable, and who must be notified.

How is this different from AI SOC analysts?

AI SOC tools enrich and triage alerts — they tell you what an alert means. InaiSec runs the room after an incident is confirmed: what the attacker accessed, across which systems, what data and customers are affected, and who you are legally obligated to notify.

Does InaiSec require a data lake?

No. Evidence is collected on demand at investigation time from the systems involved, scoped to the case. Closing the case purges collected evidence with a verifiable manifest; the record and audit trail remain.

What does the LLM actually decide?

It plans evidence retrieval and explains what evidence means. It never sees credentials, never computes notification obligations, and never decides what to investigate next — those belong to deterministic code and to people. Retrieval plans are approved by an analyst before they run.

How does InaiSec handle credentials?

Read-only, scoped credentials in an encrypted vault (AES-256-GCM), single-use intake tokens, and revocation that halts in-flight retrievals. Every call to your systems is audit-logged. The LLM never sees them.

Which regulations does it cover today?

Four, legally reviewed: California Civ. Code §1798.82, New York SHIELD §899-aa, GDPR Article 33, and SEC Form 8-K Item 1.05. Anything else in your blast radius is flagged as an explicit coverage gap with guidance to obtain local counsel — never silently dropped. Output is decision support for your counsel, not legal advice.

What is deliberately not built?

Containment and automated response — that is Phase 2, after the investigation layer earns trust with design partners. Detection and triage stay in your existing stack: incidents arrive at InaiSec confirmed.
