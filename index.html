The problem

How it is built

No data lake

Evidence is collected on demand at investigation time and is not retained beyond active investigations.

No per-system connectors

The connector discovers API surfaces dynamically — any system, no integration backlog.

Credentials stay sealed

The LLM never sees customer credentials. Access is scoped, read-only, and revocable.

Everything audit-logged

Every call to a customer system is recorded and reviewable.

Deterministic where it must be

Notification rules and timelines are rule-based. The LLM interprets evidence; it never decides obligations.

Questions about the platform, the research, or the design partner program.

What is identity-first incident response?

It is incident response that starts from the credential, account, role, and access path. The goal is to answer what the identity touched, what data may be affected, and who must be notified.

How is this different from ITDR or AI SOC tools?

ITDR and AI SOC tools help detect and validate identity threats. InaiSec does the work after validation: evidence retrieval, cross-system attack-chain tracing, blast radius, and disclosure readiness.

What is built today?

The Phase 1 platform runs today: on-demand evidence collection, credential-to-impact investigation, notification readiness across 14 regulations, case management, and controlled access to customer systems — plus the analyst dashboard. Containment is Phase 2 and is not built yet.

Does InaiSec require a data lake?

No. Evidence is collected on demand at investigation time and is not retained beyond active investigations. There is no pre-ingestion and no log lake.

How does InaiSec handle credentials?

Credentials live in a scoped vault with read-only access and revocation. The LLM never sees them. Every call to a customer system is audit-logged, and retrieval is bounded to the active investigation.

What systems are in scope first?

The current design center is identity, cloud, SaaS, and data systems such as Okta, Entra ID, Google Workspace, AWS, and Snowflake.
